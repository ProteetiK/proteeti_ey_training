1. (a) In the SmartRFP domain, "customer churn" can be defined as the failure to successfully respond to an RFP. Here, the objective is to predict whether an incoming Request for Proposal (RFP) can be accommodated by the host company with their in-company work capacity. To do this we must compute the viability of the input RFP, in addition to simply generating an RFP responses.
Problem Statement:
Develop a supervised machine learning model that predicts the likelihood of an RFP being successfully pursued based on its content, metadata, and historical proposal outcomes. The model will help proposal managers prioritize high-value RFPs, improve bid quality, and allocate resources more effectively.
Target Variable: Bid_Success (Values: Contract_awarded, Contract_rejected)
Prediction Window: The prediction is made immediately after an RFP document is uploaded and parsed, before proposal preparation begins. This gives the business sufficient time to decide whether to pursue the opportunity and allocate resources efficiently.
Unit of Analysis: The unit of analysis is one RFP document containing features such as: {Extracted requirements and evaluation criteria, Industry/domain
Contract value (if available), Submission deadline, Technical complexity, Compliance requirements}
The model should correctly identify high-probability RFPs for pursuit and help reduce time spent on low-value opportunities.
This model allows SmartRFP to provide actionable recommendations early in the proposal lifecycle, enabling better decision-making and more efficient use of proposal development resources.

1. (b) Business Metrics
- Bid Win Rate: Percentage of submitted RFPs that are successfully accepted. If SmartRFP helps identify and prioritize high-potential RFPs, the organization should see an increase in its overall proposal success rate.
- Proposal Preparation Time: Average time required to analyze an RFP and prepare an initial response draft. SmartRFP automates document analysis, requirement extraction, and draft generation, reducing manual effort and enabling teams to respond to more RFPs within deadlines.
Machine Learning Metrics
- F1-Score: The dataset might contain an imbalance between successful and unsuccessful RFPs. This metric balances precision and recall, ensuring the model performs well on both positive and negative predictions.
- ROC-AUC (Area Under the ROC Curve): This measures the model's ability to distinguish between high and low-probability RFP opportunities across different classification thresholds. A higher ROC-AUC indicates better discrimination.
High ML Metric Not Enough: A model may achieve excellent ML metrics but still fail to satisfy stakeholders if it does not add much business value. Eg. the model may accurately classify RFPs but provide recommendations that proposal managers do not trust or cannot properly interpret. Here, it will not help with higher adoption.

2. (a) Triage and Prioritization:
We should prioritize issues based on their effect on the prediction pipeline.
Priority 1: Corrupted Timestamps: This field is important because we need to determine submission deadlines and the chronological ordering of RFPs. This will affect actual business and must be fixed immediately.
Priority 2: Unseen Categories: If the last month's data contains 40 new categories that were not present during training (eg. new industry sectors or RFP types), this can reduce prediction accuracy. Still, the model can make predictions if unknown categories are handled appropriately during preprocessing. Therefore, this issue is important but less critical than fixing corrupted timestamps.

2. (b) Remediation Techniques
- Corrupted Timestamps: We must validate timestamps against known date formats, and recover timestamps from the original RFP document if available. If recovery is not possible, we must impute missing values using related fields (such as document upload date or submission deadline). We must only remove records where timestamps absolutely cannot be reconstructed.
- Unseen Categories
During preprocessing, we need to map unseen categories to a generic "Other" or "Unknown" category. We should also use encoders (eg. One-Hot Encoder) that safely process unseen values. We can also look at recording the frequency of new categories for model retraining.

4. A dropping validation score on an unseen dataset indicates that the model is not generalizing well. The three most plausible root causes:
- Data Drift: The backend dataset evolves over time as new industries, RFP formats, and compliance policies evolve. Hence, the feature distribution in the most recent data may differ significantly from the training data. This temporal drift causes the model to perform well on historical data but poorly on inputs. I would diagnose this by plotting the distribution of the important numerical features like RFP complexity, contract value, and submission deadline and comparing the frequency distribution of key categorical features like industry sector or RFP type.
- Training Data Leakage: Another possible cause is that some information unavailable at prediction time could have accidentally entered the training process. For eg., features derived from proposal outcomes. Such leakage can artificially inflate the validation score while causing poor performance on truly unseen future data. To diagnose this, we should recheck every engineered feature to ensure it is available at prediction time, and verify that preprocessing methods like encoding, scaling or imputation has been fit only on the training data.
- Overfitting of Model: The model might have fit itself too well to the historical training data instead of generalized relationships. This can happen if the model is very complex, and the dataset is relatively small. For this, we should recheck the training and validation scores, examine learning curves and evaluate performance using cross-validation with multiple temporal splits.

5. In SmartRFP, assuming only 4% of RFPs in the dataset are successful, we have to note that we end up with a highly imbalanced classification problem, making accuracy a misleading evaluation metric. In this case, a model blindly predicting every incoming request as a rejection (as it would be motivated to), will end up misclassifying all the positive cases. This is highly misleading. In the real world, this would mean that either a promising RFP will be passed on by the team or the team will waste time and resources on an unfruitful RFP. Both scenarios bring with them real cost to the company in either lost revenue or misplaced resource distribution.
Instead of accuracy, we can use:
- F1-Score: balances precision and recall, making it appropriate for imbalanced datasets.
- Precision and Recall: both metrics should be reported separately as well since they reflect different business priorities. High Recall ensures that possiblysuccessful RFPs are not rejected nd high precision ensures that proposal teams do not waste resources pursuing possibly unsuccessful opportunities.
- ROC-AUC: evaluates the model's ability to distinguish between successful and unsuccessful RFPs across different thresholds. Since the dataset is highly imbalanced, Precision-Recall AUC (PR-AUC) is more useful because it focuses on performance for the minority (positive) class.

6. Data/Feature Drift Checks
- Population Stability Index for key features: we should keep track of important features such as industry sector, contract value, and submission deadline using this. If the PSI exceeds 0.25, we can raise alarm for significant data drift. In that case, we should investigate the changes in the data, retrain the model using recent RFP data, and update the preprocessing pipeline if necessary.
- Unseen Category Rate: we should also track the percentage of new categorical values, such as new RFP types, that were not present during training. If more than 10% of incoming records contain unseen categories, we should update the category mappings or encoders and retrain the model to incorporate the new categories.
Performance Decay Checks
- F1-Score: since we are dealing with an imbalanced dataset, we should monitor the F1-score on newly labeled RFP outcomes. If the F1-score drops by more than 10% from the original production baseline, we should investigate the cause, retrain the model with updated data, and redeploy it after validation.
- Recall for Successful RFPs: This is important because missing a genuinely valuable RFP means losing a whole business opportunity. If also the recall falls below 80% of the baseline value, we should review the false negative predictions, analyze whether new RFP patterns have emerged, and retrain the model or adjust the classification threshold.
The drift checks must be run weekly and the evaluate performance metrics should be monthly. If any threshold is exceeded, the issues must be investigated, the model must be retrained with recent data and redeployed.

(a) Subgroup Analysis: If a disparity is confirmed, we should first identify the subgroup in question, whether it is the industry sector, geographic region, or organization type etc. Then, to verify the stakeholder's concern, we should compare the model's performance across each subgroup using Precision, Recall, and F1-score. In these, Recall would be the most important metric since it measures how well the model identifies truly high-value RFP opportunities for each subgroup. For eg., if the recall for public sector RFPs is much lower than for private sector RFPs, it would denote that the model is systematically missing valuable opportunities in that subgroup.
7. (b) Feature-Level Mitigation: We need to examine the training dataset to determine whether the affected subgroup is underrepresented or not. If yes then we should collect additional data for that subgroup, or use techniques like oversampling to create a more balanced dataset. We should also review the features and ensure they are relevant and do not unintentionally introduce bias. 
Model Level Mitigation: We can also try to adjust the model's decision thresholds or incorporate group-wise threshold tuning to ensure fairness across subgroups. Then we should retrain and redeploy the model so that the prediction quality does not drop.
